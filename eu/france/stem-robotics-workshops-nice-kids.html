Chapter 134 – Agglomerative vs Divisive Clustering

1. Two Directions on the Same Tree

Hierarchical clustering builds nested groups. Agglomerative works bottom-up; divisive works top-down. Both end in a hierarchy, but they travel opposite paths.

2. Agglomerative (Bottom-Up)

Each point starts as its own cluster. Closest pairs are merged repeatedly until one root remains. Linkage choices:

  • Single: nearest pair between clusters
  • Complete: farthest pair
  • Average: mean of all cross pairs
  • Ward: minimize post-merge variance

Distances are usually Euclidean or Manhattan.

3. Divisive (Top-Down)

All points start in one root cluster. Dissimilar subsets are split until leaves (or a depth stop) are reached. Captures global structure but is much more expensive to run.

4. Practical Trade-Offs

  • Divisive: heavy compute; less common in day-to-day work
  • Agglomerative: simpler, widely supported, often cleaner boundaries

5. Scikit-Learn Sketch

from sklearn.cluster import AgglomerativeClustering, BisectingKMeans
from sklearn.datasets import make_blobs
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

X, _ = make_blobs(n_samples=200, centers=3, cluster_std=0.9, random_state=42)

agg = AgglomerativeClustering(n_clusters=3, linkage="ward")
agg_labels = agg.fit_predict(X)

div = BisectingKMeans(n_clusters=3, random_state=42)
div_labels = div.fit_predict(X)

fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(9, 4))
axes[0].scatter(X[:, 0], X[:, 1], c=agg_labels, cmap="viridis", s=30)
axes[0].set_title("Agglomerative")
axes[1].scatter(X[:, 0], X[:, 1], c=div_labels, cmap="viridis", s=30)
axes[1].set_title("Divisive")
plt.tight_layout(); plt.show()

On synthetic blobs, agglomerative often separates boundaries cleanly; divisive may misassign a few boundary points.

Lesson Q&A

Agglomerative starts with single-point clusters and merges upward. Divisive starts with one root and splits downward until leaves or a depth stop.

Single (nearest pair), complete (farthest), average (mean of pairs), and Ward (minimize variance). Distances are typically Euclidean or Manhattan.

It is cheaper to run, well supported (e.g. AgglomerativeClustering), and often yields cleaner boundaries than divisive methods on the same data.
