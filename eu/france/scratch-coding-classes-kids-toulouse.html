Chapter 133 – Hierarchical Clustering

1. Nested Groups Without Pre-Set K

Hierarchical clustering builds a hierarchy of nested groups. Unlike K-Means or K-NN, it does not require choosing K in advance—so you avoid expert guesses and trial-and-error over many K values.

2. Dendrograms and XAI

Results are shown as a dendrogram (tree): root = one all-inclusive cluster; leaves = individual points; branches = merges or splits. That visual structure makes the method a form of Explainable AI—you can inspect how groups formed and which distances drove merges.

3. Fine vs Coarse; Two Approaches

  • Fine clusters: small, pure groups.
  • Coarse clusters: broader merges for a big-picture view.
  • Agglomerative (bottom-up): each point starts alone; similar groups merge until one root remains.
  • Divisive (top-down): one root is split repeatedly down to single points.

4. Sensitivities and Scale

Choice of distance (Euclidean vs Manhattan) and linkage (single, complete, average) changes the tree. Grid search can tune those hyperparameters. Works well on small-to-medium data; full pairwise tree building is expensive on very large sets.

5. Applications

  • Gene expression grouping in biology
  • Customer segments and churn risk tiers
  • Document hierarchies for large text collections
