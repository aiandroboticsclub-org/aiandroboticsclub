Chapter 115 – Support Vector Machines (SVM) Explained for Beginners

1. What SVM Does

SVM is a strong supervised algorithm used mainly for classification (and also for regression). Instead of any separating boundary, it finds the optimal hyperplane that maximizes the margin between classes. It handles high-dimensional spaces well and can model non-linear data with kernels.

2. Hyperplanes by Dimension

DimensionHyperplane looks like
1DA point on a line
2DA straight line (\(w \cdot x + b = 0\))
3DA flat plane
>3DA generalized hyperplane

3. Support Vectors and Margin

  • Support vectors are the points closest to the boundary. Only they determine the hyperplane’s position and orientation. Removing far-away points does not change the solution.
  • Margin is the distance from the hyperplane to those nearest points. SVM maximizes this margin for better generalization and less sensitivity to noise.

4. Math in Brief

  • Hyperplane: \(w \cdot x + b = 0\)
  • Objective: minimize \(\frac{1}{2}\|w\|^2\) (equivalent to maximizing margin \(2/\|w\|\))
  • Constraint: \(y_i (w \cdot x_i + b) \ge 1\) (equality holds for support vectors)

5. High Dimensions and Kernels

SVM works well when there are many features. When data is not linearly separable, kernel functions map it so a curved boundary can still separate the classes.

Lesson Q&A

It is the decision boundary that separates classes. In 1D it is a point, in 2D a line, in 3D a plane, and in higher dimensions a generalized hyperplane.

Support vectors are the points closest to the hyperplane and fully determine it. Removing points that are farther away leaves the hyperplane (and \(w\), \(b\)) unchanged.

The margin is the distance from the hyperplane to the nearest support vectors. Larger margins generalize better and are less noise-sensitive. SVM maximizes margin by minimizing \(\frac{1}{2}\|w\|^2\) subject to \(y_i (w \cdot x_i + b) \ge 1\).
