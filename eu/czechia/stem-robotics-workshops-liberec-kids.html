Chapter 120 – K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) Explained

1. What Is KNN?

K-Nearest Neighbors is a simple supervised algorithm for classification and regression. Its idea is "birds of a feather flock together": a new point is labeled by looking at its closest neighbors in feature space.

  • Lazy learner: no fitted equation or weights—training data stays in memory and is queried at prediction time.
  • Explainable: every decision can be traced to the actual neighbors used.
  • Dual use: majority vote for classes; mean of neighbor targets for continuous values.

2. Advantages and Uses

  • Easy to understand; non-parametric (no distribution assumptions).
  • Works with mixed numerical and categorical features when distances are defined carefully.
  • Examples: handwritten digit recognition; house price estimates from similar nearby properties.

3. Four-Step Workflow

  1. Choose k — usually an odd number for classification to avoid ties (e.g. 3, 5, 7).
  2. Compute distances from the query to every training point (Euclidean or Manhattan).
  3. Sort distances and keep the top-k nearest neighbors.
  4. Predict — majority vote (classification) or mean of targets (regression).

4. Distance Metrics

  • Euclidean: straight-line distance — √(Σ (xᵢ − yᵢ)²).
  • Manhattan: grid path — sum of absolute differences.

5. Concrete Examples

Classification (k=5): Neighbors vote Red, Red, Blue, Red, Blue → Red wins 3–2.

Regression (k=3 house prices): $250k, $270k, $260k → predicted price $260k.

Lesson Q&A

It does not learn parameters; it keeps the training set and compares a new point to stored examples at inference. Predictions are explained by the actual neighbors and their labels or values—"birds of a feather flock together."

Odd k (3, 5, 7…) avoids 2–2 style ties in majority vote. Distances are usually Euclidean (straight-line) or Manhattan (sum of absolute coordinate differences).

Classification: majority vote among the k neighbors. Regression: mean of the k neighbors' continuous target values (e.g. average of three nearby house prices).
