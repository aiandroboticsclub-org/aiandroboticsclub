Chapter 139 – PCA: Eigenvectors & Eigenvalues

1. Why These Concepts Matter

PCA rests on linear algebra. From the covariance matrix of standardized data we extract eigenvectors (directions) and eigenvalues (how much variance sits on each direction). That pair becomes the principal components.

2. Definitions

  • Eigenvector (v): direction that does not change under the matrix transform—becomes a PC axis
  • Eigenvalue (λ): scalar stretch along that vector—equals variance captured by the component

Core equation: A v = λ v, where A is the covariance matrix.

3. PCA Workflow

  1. Standardize (mean 0, variance 1)
  2. Build covariance matrix A
  3. Solve for eigenvalues & eigenvectors
  4. Sort by λ descending
  5. Explained variance ratio = each λ / sum of all λ

4. Useful Properties

  • Eigenvectors from a symmetric covariance matrix are orthogonal → uncorrelated PCs
  • Sum of eigenvalues = total variance of the data
  • Keep large-λ components; drop near-zero ones to compress with little loss

5. NumPy Sketch

import numpy as np

data_std = (data - data.mean(0)) / data.std(0)
cov = np.cov(data_std, rowvar=False)
eigenvalues, eigenvectors = np.linalg.eig(cov)

idx = np.argsort(eigenvalues)[::-1]
eigenvalues = eigenvalues[idx]
eigenvectors = eigenvectors[:, idx]

ratio = eigenvalues / eigenvalues.sum()
print("PC1 variance share:", ratio[0])

Typical demo output: λ ≈ [3.85, 1.34] → about 74% variance on PC1 and 26% on PC2.

Lesson Q&A

An eigenvector is a direction unchanged by the covariance transform; it becomes a PC axis. An eigenvalue is the stretch (variance) along that direction. They satisfy A v = λ v.

Without scaling, large-magnitude features dominate variance and bias the eigenvectors. Mean-0 / unit-variance standardization keeps every column on equal footing.

Divide each eigenvalue by the sum of all eigenvalues. In the demo, ≈74% of variance sits on PC1 and ≈26% on PC2—so keeping only the first component already retains most structure.
