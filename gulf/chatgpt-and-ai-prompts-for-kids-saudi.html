What a good prompt does

A good prompt states the goal, the audience, and constraints. “Explain photosynthesis for a 12-year-old in five bullet points” is clearer than “tell me about plants.” Children learn that vague prompts produce vague or invented details.

Verification as a non-negotiable habit

Students should cross-check important facts with trusted sources. Mentors can require a two-source rule for science claims. The model is a drafting assistant, not an authority.

Privacy

Do not paste personal data, school credentials or private family details into public tools. Supervised accounts and clear house rules matter.
