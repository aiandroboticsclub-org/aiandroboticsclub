Combined coding and robotics classes help students in Askari 4 see software and hardware as one system. Code reads sensor values; decisions in code change motors or lights. The feedback is immediate and motivating when the logic is sound.

Sensors, actuators and control

Ultrasonic or light sensors illustrate input. LEDs and motors illustrate output. Conditional statements connect the two. Students learn that reliable behaviour depends on clear thresholds and careful testing—not on guessing.

Closing the loop between code and motion

When a simulated LED turns on because a light sensor crossed a threshold, students witness a closed loop. They adjust the threshold and watch behaviour change. That direct feedback is difficult to obtain from worksheets alone.

Instructors emphasise naming and comments in code even at beginner level. Future readers—including the student next week—need to understand intent. Safety talks cover unplugging power before changing wires, avoiding short circuits and never experimenting with mains electricity.

Families who enjoy the pathway can discuss optional hardware later. Until then, simulation keeps learning continuous and low-risk.
