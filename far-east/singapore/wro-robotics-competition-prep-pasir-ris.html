1. Making Sense of Data with Descriptive Statistics

Descriptive statistics summarize structure and check that values make sense before modeling. Useful tools include:

  • df.describe() — multi-row summary across numeric features
  • value_counts() — frequency of distinct values
  • quantile() — split distributions into probability portions
  • skew() — whether the curve leans left or right

Core ideas: central tendency (mean, median), dispersion (std, quartiles, IQR), shape (skewness), and simple validity checks (e.g. Q3 > Q1).

2. Quantiles and the Interquartile Range (IQR)

Quartiles each cover about 25% of observations:

  • Q1 (25%) — lower quarter boundary
  • Q2 (50% / median) — middle of the data
  • Q3 (75%) — upper quarter boundary

IQR = Q3 − Q1 measures spread of the middle 50% of values.

3. Skewness

  • Right-skewed (positive) — long right tail; mean > median; .skew() > 0
  • Left-skewed (negative) — long left tail; mean < median; .skew() < 0

4. Practical Example: E-Commerce Revenue

import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv("ecommerce_orders.csv", parse_dates=["Order Date"])
print("Dataset Shape:", df.shape)

stats = df[["Unit Price", "Revenue"]].describe()
print(stats)

q1 = df["Revenue"].quantile(0.25)
q2 = df["Revenue"].quantile(0.50)
q3 = df["Revenue"].quantile(0.75)
print(f"Q1: {q1}, Median: {q2}, Q3: {q3}")

print("Mean:", df["Revenue"].mean())
print("Median:", df["Revenue"].median())
print("Skew:", df["Revenue"].skew())

print(df.groupby("Product Category")["Revenue"].mean())

iqr = q3 - q1
print("IQR:", iqr)
print("Q3 > Q1:", q3 > q1)
print("IQR > 0:", iqr > 0)

Illustrative results on a 5,000-row sample: Q1 ≈ 354, median ≈ 796, Q3 ≈ 1,515; mean ≈ 1,021 (above median → right skew ≈ 1.017); IQR ≈ 1,161.

5. Group Means and Validation

groupby(...).mean() compares average revenue by product category. Checking Q3 > Q1 and IQR > 0 confirms the quartile order is sensible.

Lesson Q&A

Quantiles split data into equal probability portions. Quartiles (Q1, Q2/median, Q3) each cover about 25%. IQR = Q3 − Q1 is the spread of the middle 50% of the data.

Right-skewed: mean > median and .skew() is positive (example: revenue mean ~1,021 vs median ~796, skew ~1.017). Left-skewed: mean < median and .skew() is negative.

Example values: Q1 354, median 796, Q3 1,515; IQR = 1,161. Confirming Q3 > Q1 and IQR > 0 shows the distribution boundaries are ordered as expected.
