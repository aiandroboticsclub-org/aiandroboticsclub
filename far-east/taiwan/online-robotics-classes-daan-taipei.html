1. Shape, Spread, Skew

Distribution plots summarize continuous variables as bins, smooth densities, or cumulative curves—without needing a scatter of every row.

  • sns.histplot — discrete bins and counts (stack / layer / dodge)
  • sns.kdeplot — smooth kernel density (no bins)
  • sns.ecdfplot — cumulative proportion from 0 to 1

2. Useful Arguments

  • multiple: stack, layer, or dodge groups
  • stat: count, density, or probability
  • bins: number of intervals for histograms
  • fill: shade under KDE curves

3. Treatment Cost Histogram

import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import seaborn as sns

df = pd.read_csv("healthcare_patient_analytics_seaborn.csv")

plt.figure(figsize=(9, 5))
sns.histplot(data=df, x="Treatment Cost", bins=30)
plt.title("Treatment Cost Distribution (30 Bins)")
plt.xlabel("Treatment Cost ($)")
plt.ylabel("Count")
plt.show()

Mid-range costs dominate; extremes are rarer.

5. KDE of Recovery Score

plt.figure(figsize=(9, 5))
sns.kdeplot(
    data=df,
    x="Recovery Score",
    hue="Visit Type",
    fill=True
)
plt.title("Recovery Score Density Distribution by Visit Type")
plt.show()

Routine visits often show a sharper, higher density peak than Emergency.

6. ECDF of Length of Stay

plt.figure(figsize=(9, 5))
sns.ecdfplot(
    data=df,
    x="Length of Stay (Days)",
    hue="Visit Type"
)
plt.title("Cumulative Length of Stay (Days) by Visit Type")
plt.ylabel("Proportion")
plt.show()

In the example, about 80% of stays fall at or below 6 days.

Lesson Q&A

histplot bins data into discrete bars (count/density/probability) and can stack groups. kdeplot draws a continuous density curve without bins; fill=True shades under the curve.

ECDF accumulates proportions as x increases, rising monotonically from 0 to 1. A horizontal value such as day 6 paired with proportion 0.8 means 80% of cases are at or below that stay length.

Treatment costs peaked in middle bins (moderate costs most common). Stacked readmission-risk histograms let Routine vs Emergency shares be compared across risk levels on one chart.
