1. Purpose of an EDA Report

After cleaning, summarizing, detecting outliers, and measuring correlations, findings are packaged into a short EDA report for stakeholders. It turns raw tables into actionable insights and lets the client (domain SME) validate interpretation before modeling decisions are locked in.

2. Narrative + Visual Evidence

A strong report mixes concise bullets with charts/tables: quality issues, distributions, relationships, and recommended fixes. Keep it short—typically a focused 6–8 bullet executive summary—rather than dozens of pages.

3. Six Summary Dimensions

  1. Data quality & missingness — rates and proposed remedies (e.g. median impute or drop)
  2. Feature distributions — skewness and transforms (e.g. log1p for right-skewed Revenue)
  3. Attribute relationships — correlation strengths that match (or challenge) business intuition
  4. Categorical concentration — share of orders/revenue in top categories
  5. Outliers & anomalies — extreme records to keep or remove with client input
  6. Temporal / seasonal trends — cycles such as Q4 holiday spikes

4. Practical Snippets

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd
import matplotlib.pyplot as plt

df = pd.read_csv("retail_dataset.csv")

missing_rate = df["Customer_Rating"].isna().mean() * 100
print(f"Missing Customer_Rating: {missing_rate:.1f}%")

print("Revenue skew:", df["Revenue"].skew())
df["Revenue_Log"] = np.log1p(df["Revenue"])

print("Qty–Revenue corr:", df["Quantity"].corr(df["Revenue"]))
print("Top-3 category share:", df["Category"].value_counts(normalize=True).head(3).sum())

Pair metrics with simple plots (e.g. raw vs log Revenue histograms) so stakeholders see the problem and the proposed fix.
