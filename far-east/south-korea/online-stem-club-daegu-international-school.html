1. Why Bar Charts?

Bars compare categories after aggregation. On large tables (e.g. 5,000 rows), group first so a few bars represent totals by product line, region, etc.—not one bar per row.

2. Simple Bar Chart

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import pandas as pd

df = pd.read_csv("ecommerce_orders.csv")
category_revenue = df.groupby("Product Category")["Revenue"].sum()

fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 5))
ax.bar(category_revenue.index, category_revenue.values)
ax.set_title("Total Revenue by Product Category")
ax.set_xlabel("Product Category")
ax.set_ylabel("Revenue ($)")
plt.show()

Thousands of rows collapse into one bar per category (e.g. Electronics highest in a sample).

3. Grouped Bars (.unstack())

grouped = df.groupby(["Product Category", "Region"])["Revenue"].sum()
unstacked = grouped.unstack()
unstacked.plot(kind="bar", figsize=(10, 4))
plt.title("Revenue by Category Across Regions")
plt.xlabel("Product Category")
plt.ylabel("Revenue ($)")
plt.show()

.unstack() pivots the inner index (Region) into columns so each category shows side-by-side regional bars and a legend.

4. Stacked Bars

unstacked.plot(kind="bar", stacked=True, figsize=(10, 5))
plt.title("Stacked Revenue by Category and Region")
plt.xlabel("Product Category")
plt.ylabel("Total Accumulated Revenue ($)")
plt.show()

Stacking shows category total (bar height) and each region’s share within that total. Grouped bars are better for direct region-to-region comparison; stacked bars emphasize composition.

Lesson Q&A

Raw rows would flood the chart. Group (e.g. sum Revenue by Product Category), then pass category labels and values to ax.bar() for a readable summary.

After a multi-index groupby, .unstack() turns the inner level (e.g. Region) into columns so Pandas can draw side-by-side bars per category with a color legend.

Grouped: bars side-by-side for easy cross-region comparison. Stacked: segments piled in one bar to show totals and each segment’s share of that total.
